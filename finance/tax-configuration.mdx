In Fail Fast you do not type taxes. When you save a document, the system looks at it, applies the country's tax rules, and writes the result. Nobody keys in 19 % VAT or a 4 % withholding.

Your job is to tell the system who your company is, who your stakeholders are, what you buy and sell, and which accounts the taxes post to. With that, every document settles itself. If your company is small, almost all of that resolves on its own: see [Cases by company size](/finance/tax-company-size-cases).

## How Fail Fast decides which taxes apply

For each line of a document, the system looks at three things:

| What it looks at | The question it answers | Where you configure it |
|---|---|---|
| **Who the stakeholder is** | Do they file income tax? Are they a legal entity? Do they self-withhold? Are they in the simple regime? | The **Tax profile** (**Perfil fiscal**) of your company, supplier, or customer. See [Tax profile](/finance/tax-fiscal-profile) |
| **What the product is** | Is it a service, fees, taxable goods, an excluded good? | The tax category and the withholding category of the **product type**. See [Products](/finance/tax-products) |
| **Where it happens** | In which municipality? That decides ICA and ReteICA | The **location of each line** of the document. See [CIIU, ICA activity, and ReteICA](/finance/tax-ica-activity) |

It also uses the document's **operation date**: the rate, the minimum, and the value of the UVT are the ones in force that day. A 2025 invoice that you settle again today uses 2025 values.

Each country rule reads this data through conditions. When a condition is not met, the tax does not apply and the system writes down the reason. When nobody answered a piece of data the rule needs, the system does not assume it: it tells you. See [Verify taxes](/finance/tax-verify).

### An example with numbers

You buy cleaning services for **1,000,000** from a company that files income tax, on October 15, 2026, with the line located in Medellín. Your company is an income withholding agent and an ICA withholding agent in Medellín.

| Tax | Base | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAT | 1,000,000 | 19 % | **190,000** |
| Income withholding for services | 1,000,000 | 4 % | **40,000** |
| ICA withholding | 1,000,000 | 1.8 per thousand | **1,800** |

You pay the supplier `1,000,000 + 190,000 − 40,000 − 1,800 = 1,148,200`.

<Note>
Withholding minimums are in UVT, not in pesos. The system does the math with the UVT of the document's date, so you do not have to update anything each January. The minimum is compared with the sum of the lines of the same rule in the document.
</Note>

There are more examples, with the accountant's figures, in [Worked examples](/finance/tax-worked-examples).

## What Fail Fast does and what is up to you

| Fail Fast loads and maintains it | You configure it |
|---|---|
| The country's taxes and their rules: rate, minimum base, municipality, validity, and conditions | Your company's **Tax profile** (once) |
| Bracketed rate ranges and the tax calendar | Your company's **principal CIIU** and ICA activity per municipality |
| The data a rule can ask about the company, the stakeholder, and the product | The **Tax profile** of each stakeholder, only what their rules need |
| Tax categories: taxable, excluded, exempt, reduced rate | The tax category and withholding category of each **product type**, and the fiscal attribute of products that are the exception |
| The table of ICA activities per municipality, with its rate | The **Tax account map**: which account each tax goes to |
| The calculation, the explanation, and the accounting entry | **Tax rule override**, only if your company needs them |

<Warning>
Do not modify the country's rates or rules: they are the same for every company and are read-only. What is different in your company is recorded as a **tax rule override**. See [Tax rule override](/finance/tax-rule-exceptions).
</Warning>

## Configure taxes step by step

Follow this order: you cannot post a tax that is not calculated yet, nor calculate anything on a product that does not say what it is.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete your company's tax profile">
    Open your own company's stakeholder record and go to the **Tax profile** (**Perfil fiscal**) table. To the system your company is one more stakeholder. Its responsibilities are taken from the RUT first; answer the ones that are missing. The rows, their origin, and what happens if you leave them empty are in [Tax profile](/finance/tax-fiscal-profile).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your CIIU and your ICA activity">
    Register your company's CIIU, mark the principal one, and, if you operate in a municipality that has an activity table, declare your ICA activity there. See [CIIU, ICA activity, and ReteICA](/finance/tax-ica-activity).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the tax profile of your suppliers and customers">
    Create the stakeholder with their document and name. The fastest way is to upload their RUT and let AI fill it in: see [Load the RUT of a stakeholder](#load-the-rut-of-a-stakeholder). After that you only look at the rows the system marks as missing. A person with an ID card who buys at your shop needs nothing.

    The stakeholder's **location** is not just contact data: with it the system knows whether they are from abroad, and that decides whether the purchase carries VAT. The stakeholder cannot be saved without it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify your product types">
    This is the step that is forgotten most. The tax category and the withholding category go in the **product type**: they are what connects a line to a tax rule. Without them the document saves, the totals add up, and no tax appears. See [Products](/finance/tax-products).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say which account each tax goes to">
    In the **Tax account map** (**Mapa de cuentas de impuestos**) you say, for each tax and each role it plays, which accounting account it posts to. See [Tax account map](/finance/tax-account-map).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Register your exceptions, if you need them">
    When a country rule does not apply to your company, or applies at another rate, register an exception with its validity, scope, and reason. Having no exceptions is normal. See [Tax rule override](/finance/tax-rule-exceptions).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify with the simulator">
    Before invoicing, try an operation in the **Tax simulator** (**Simulador fiscal**). See [Verify taxes](/finance/tax-verify).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Load the RUT of a stakeholder

Instead of typing the tax profile, you can upload the stakeholder's RUT and let AI read it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the stakeholder record">
    Open or create the supplier, customer, or stakeholder in its form. RUT reading is available on the form, not in the table view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the RUT">
    From the AI button on the form's toolbar, choose **Read RUT** and upload the file you download from the DIAN, in PDF, PNG, or JPEG, up to 10 MB.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what AI filled in">
    AI fills in the document number, the verification digit, the name or company name, the trade name, the email, the phone, the economic activities (CIIU), and the tax responsibilities. Fields filled by AI are marked. **Nothing is saved yet**: review and correct whatever needs it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Choose **Confirm the tax data from the RUT**. The responsibilities are saved into the **Tax profile** with origin **from the RUT**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

- If you add or remove a responsibility before confirming, it is saved as written by a person and takes precedence over the RUT.
- A value that came from the RUT is not edited in the **Tax profile**: you change it in the stakeholder's tax responsibilities.
- If the RUT belongs to a different stakeholder, or a stakeholder with that document already exists, the system tells you and does not merge the data.

## Settle a supplier invoice

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the invoice">
    In supplier invoices, create a new one and fill in the header. These fields decide the taxes:
    1. **Operation date**: decides which rate and which UVT are used.
    2. **Line location**: decides the municipality and with it ICA and ReteICA.
    3. **Supplier**: brings in their tax profile. They have to be active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the lines">
    Add the products or services with their quantity and amount. The line's tax category arrives on its own from the product type. If it comes up empty, classify the product type before saving. Choose the line location: it is the municipality where the purchase happens.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    On save, Fail Fast settles every tax on the document. It is all or nothing: either all of them are calculated or none is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the taxes tab">
    You will find three grids, in this order:
    - **Summary**: one line per tax, with its operation (tax, withholding, higher value), base, rate, and amount. This is what goes to accounting.
    - **Detail**: the same, line by line, with the **rule** that applied. A tax with no rule was not calculated by the system: someone typed it in.
    - **Not applied**: the rules that were evaluated and did not apply, each with its reason written out. This is the grid that explains a withholding you expected and did not get.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post it">
    With the tax account map configured, the invoice posts with its taxes in the accounts you defined.
  </Step>
</Steps>
