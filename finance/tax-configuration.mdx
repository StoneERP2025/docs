In Fail Fast you do not type taxes. When you save an invoice, the system looks at the document, applies the country's tax rules, and writes the result. Nobody keys in 19 % VAT or a 4 % withholding.

Your job is to tell the system who your company is, who your stakeholders are, what you buy and sell, and which accounts the taxes post to. With that, every invoice settles itself.

## How Fail Fast decides which taxes apply

For each invoice line, the system looks at three things:

| What it looks at | The question it answers | Where you configure it |
|---|---|---|
| **Who the stakeholder is** | Do they file income tax? Are they a legal entity? Do they self-withhold? Are they on the simple regime? | The **tax profile** of the supplier or customer |
| **What the product is** | Is it a service, professional fees, taxable goods, an excluded item? | The **tax categories** of the product type |
| **Where it happens** | In which municipality? That decides ICA | The **branch office** on the invoice |

It also uses the document's **operation date**: the rate, the threshold, and the UVT value are the ones in force that day. A 2025 invoice you re-settle today uses 2025 values.

### An example with numbers

You buy cleaning services for **1,000,000** from a company that files income tax, on 15 October 2026, with a branch in Medellín. Your company is an income withholding agent and an ICA withholding agent in Medellín.

| Tax | Base | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|
| VAT | 1,000,000 | 19 % | **190,000** |
| Income withholding on services | 1,000,000 | 4 % | **40,000** |
| ICA withholding | 1,000,000 | 1.8 per thousand | **1,800** |

You pay the supplier `1,000,000 + 190,000 − 40,000 − 1,800 = 1,148,200`.

<Note>
Withholding thresholds are expressed in UVT, not in pesos. The system does the arithmetic with the UVT of the document's date, so you do not have to update anything every January. The threshold is compared against the sum of the lines under the same rule in the document.
</Note>

## What Fail Fast does and what is up to you

| Fail Fast does this for you | You configure this once |
|---|---|
| Keeps the country's rates, thresholds, and validity periods | Your company's tax profile |
| Picks the rule that applies to each line | The tax profile of your suppliers and customers (or loads it from their RUT) |
| Calculates base, rate, and amount | The tax categories of your product types |
| Explains why a tax did not apply | Which account each tax posts to |
| Posts the accounting entry | Exceptions, only if your company needs them |

<Warning>
Do not modify the country's rates or rules: they are the same for every company and are read-only. Whatever is different in your company is recorded as an **exception**.
</Warning>

## Configure taxes step by step

Follow this order: you cannot post a tax that is not calculated yet, nor calculate anything on a product that does not say what it is.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete your company's tax profile">
    Open your own company's stakeholder record and go to the **Tax profile** grid. To the system your company is one more stakeholder: if nobody answered its responsibilities, it is nobody for tax purposes.

    | Answer **Yes** if your company is… | If you leave it unanswered |
    |---|---|
    | **VAT responsible** | The VAT on your purchases is not deducted: it goes to cost |
    | **Income withholding agent** | You withhold income tax from no supplier |
    | **VAT withholding agent** | There is no VAT withholding on any invoice |
    | **ICA withholding agent in the municipality** | You do not withhold ICA in that municipality. One row per municipality |
    | **Income self-withholder** | Your customers withhold from you instead of you self-withholding |

    The grid has more than one page: go through all of them before calling this step done.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the tax profile of your suppliers and customers">
    Create the stakeholder with their document and name, and answer their **Tax profile**. The fastest way is to upload their RUT and let AI fill it in: see [Load the RUT of a stakeholder](#load-the-rut-of-a-stakeholder).

    The data that changes a purchase invoice the most:

    | Supplier data | What it changes |
    |---|---|
    | **VAT responsible** | If they are not, the line settles with no VAT and there is no VAT withholding |
    | **Income tax filer** | Picks the rate: 2.5 % or 3.5 % on goods, 4 % or 6 % on services |
    | **Is a legal entity** | Picks the professional fees rate: 11 % legal entity, 10 % individual |
    | **Income self-withholder** | You do not withhold income tax: they self-withhold |
    | **Simple tax regime** | You withhold neither income tax nor ICA |

    The stakeholder's **location** is not just contact data: it is how the system knows whether they are abroad, and that decides whether the purchase carries VAT. The stakeholder is not saved without it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Classify your product types">
    This is the step most often forgotten. The tax category does not go on each product: it goes on the **product type**, and it is what connects an invoice line to a tax rule. Without it the invoice saves, the totals add up, and no tax appears.

    | Field | What it decides |
    |---|---|
    | **VAT category** | Which VAT or consumption tax what you buy or sell triggers. It is required |
    | **Withholding category** | Which income withholding applies. Without it there is no withholding |
    | **Fixed asset** | On a purchase, the VAT goes to the asset's cost |
    | **Own consumption** | On a purchase, the VAT goes to expense |

    Some examples of **Withholding category** (with the 2026 UVT):

    | If you buy… | Pick | Withholding |
    |---|---|---|
    | Services in general | General services | 4 % if they file income tax, 6 % if not · from 104,748 |
    | Professional fees and commissions | Fees and commissions | 11 % legal entity, 10 % individual · no threshold |
    | Goods | Purchases of goods | 2.5 % if they file income tax, 3.5 % if not · from 523,740 |
    | Rent of premises or a warehouse | Rental of real estate | 3.5 % · from 523,740 |
    | Freight transport | Freight transport | 1 % · from 104,748 |

    And of **VAT category**:

    | If you buy or sell… | Pick | What it triggers |
    |---|---|---|
    | Ordinary taxable goods | Taxed at the general rate | VAT 19 % |
    | A reduced-rate good or service | Taxed at the 5 percent rate | VAT 5 % |
    | An exempt good | VAT exempt | VAT 0 %, deductible |
    | An excluded good | VAT excluded | No VAT |
    | Restaurant, café, or bar | Restaurant, café, bar | Consumption tax 8 % |

    Pick by **what the thing is**, not by the accounting account you post it to.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say which account each tax posts to">
    In **Tax accounts** you say, for each tax and each role it plays, which accounting account it posts to. The minimum to start buying:

    | Tax | Account role |
    |---|---|
    | VAT | **Deductible**, for the VAT you pay and later deduct |
    | VAT | **Higher cost value**, for the VAT you do not deduct |
    | Income withholding | **Withholding applied**, what you withhold from the supplier and owe the DIAN |
    | ICA withholding | **Withholding applied**, the same before the municipality |
    | VAT withholding | **Withholding applied**, only if you are a VAT withholding agent |

    Leave the **zone** empty so the row applies country-wide. Fill it in only when you need a different account per municipality, which is the norm for ICA.

    Accounts that carry taxes must have **Require tax** set to **Yes** along with their tax concept. See [Create an account](/finance/accounting#create-an-account).

    <Tip>
    A missing row does not cost you the invoice. The tax is calculated and stored; what is missing is the account to build the entry. Add the row and post it again.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record your exceptions, if you need any">
    When a country rule does not apply to your company, or applies at a different rate, record an exception with its validity period, its scope, and its reason:

    | Exception | Example |
    |---|---|
    | **Do not apply this rule** | "This withholding does not apply to me" |
    | **Apply a different rate** | "I withhold the agreed rate from this supplier" |

    The emptier the scope, the broader it is: with no stakeholder it applies to everyone, with no zone it applies country-wide. The reason is required because the auditor reads it.

    Having no exceptions is normal. If you have many, something in the tax profile or the categories is probably answered wrong.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Load the RUT of a stakeholder

Instead of typing the tax profile, you can upload the stakeholder's RUT and let AI read it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the stakeholder record">
    Open or create the supplier, customer, or stakeholder in its form. RUT reading is available on the form, not in the table view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the RUT">
    From the AI button on the form's toolbar, choose **Read RUT** and upload the file you download from the DIAN, in PDF, PNG, or JPEG, up to 10 MB.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what AI filled in">
    AI fills in the document number, the verification digit, the name or company name, the trade name, the email, the phone, the economic activities (CIIU), and the tax responsibilities. Fields filled by AI are marked. **Nothing is saved yet**: review and correct whatever needs it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Choose **Confirm the tax data from the RUT**. The responsibilities are saved into the **Tax profile** with origin **from the RUT**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

- If you add or remove a responsibility before confirming, it is saved as written by a person and takes precedence over the RUT.
- A value that came from the RUT is not edited in the **Tax profile**: you change it in the stakeholder's tax responsibilities.
- If the RUT belongs to a different stakeholder, or a stakeholder with that document already exists, the system tells you and does not merge the data.

## Settle a supplier invoice

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the invoice">
    In supplier invoices, create a new one and fill in the header. Three fields decide the taxes:
    1. **Operation date**: decides which rate and which UVT are used.
    2. **Branch office**: decides the municipality and with it the ICA.
    3. **Supplier**: brings in their tax profile. They have to be active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the lines">
    Add the products or services with their quantity and amount. The line's tax category arrives on its own from the product type. If it comes up empty, classify the product type before saving.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    On save, Fail Fast settles every tax on the document. It is all or nothing: either all of them are calculated or none is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the taxes tab">
    You will find three grids, in this order:
    - **Summary**: one line per tax, with its operation (tax, withholding, higher value), base, rate, and amount. This is what goes to accounting.
    - **Detail**: the same, line by line, with the **rule** that applied. A tax with no rule was not calculated by the system: someone typed it in.
    - **Not applied**: the rules that were evaluated and did not apply, each with its reason written out. This is the grid that explains a withholding you expected and did not get.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Post it">
    With the tax accounts configured, the invoice posts with its taxes in the accounts you defined.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why a tax did not come out

Always start with the **not applied** grid: the reason is written there.

| What you see | What happened | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The invoice settles no tax at all | The product type has no tax category | Classify the product type |
| It settles VAT but withholds nothing | Your company is not listed as a withholding agent, or the supplier self-withholds or is on the simple regime | Review both tax profiles |
| VAT comes out as cost instead of deductible | Your company is not listed as VAT responsible | Complete your company's tax profile |
| It withholds at the higher rate | Nobody answered whether the supplier files income tax | Answer **Income tax filer** on the supplier |
| It withholds less on small invoices | The base did not reach the UVT threshold | Nothing: the reason carries both figures |
| The tax is calculated but the invoice does not post | The account for that tax is missing | Add the row in **Tax accounts** |
| A tax with no rule in the detail | Someone typed it in by hand | Review the line |

<Note>
When nobody answered a value a rule needs, the system does not assume it: it leaves the tax under **not applied** with the message "nobody answered…" and tells you which value to record. The exception is benefits the stakeholder has to evidence, such as the special regime or self-withholding: until they are recorded, the withholding is applied.
</Note>

The not-applied grid is long by design: it shows every rule that was evaluated, including the ones outside your line of business. That is not an error.
