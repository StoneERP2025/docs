Accounting is the general ledger everything in Fail Fast posts to. Sales, purchases, treasury, inventory, and the rest of the operational modules generate their accounting entries here, and you complete the picture with manual entries, period closes, and reports.

## Where to find it

Accounting pages appear in the **Programs** section of the sidebar when the finance module is enabled for your workspace. Access to entries, closes, and reports depends on your role and permissions.

## Key concepts

- **Chart of accounts** — a hierarchical account tree organized by account classes, based on the Colombian PUC structure. Every entry line points to an account in this chart.
- **Accounting books** — you can keep multiple books, so a single transaction is represented in each book under its own account structure. See [Multi-book accounting](/finance/multi-book).
- **Journal entries** — the movements themselves: each entry carries detail lines and their tax details.
- **Balances** — account balances built from posted entries, per period.
- **Deferrals and amortizations** — spread a value across periods instead of recognizing it all at once.

## Your chart of accounts

The chart of accounts is organized by code. Every digit you add goes one level down: class `2` (liabilities) contains group `21`, which contains account `2101`, and so on down to the account you post to.

- **General chart and your own accounts.** The first levels come from your country's general chart and are the same for every company. Below them you create your company's own accounts.
- **Ledger account.** Groups other accounts and adds up what happens in them. It receives no postings.
- **Auxiliary account.** The account you post to. It cannot have accounts below it.
- **The account it adds up into.** The ledger account — yours or from the general chart — directly above an account, which totals its values.

You can chain several of your own ledger accounts before reaching the auxiliary one: a level 5 ledger account, a level 6 one, and the level 7 auxiliary.

When you create your workspace, Fail Fast loads an initial chart of accounts into it. From there you add, adjust, or deactivate accounts.

## Create an account

You do not need to be an accountant to create an account. The form works out the technical parts from the code you type.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the account form">
    On the **Account** page of the accounting configuration, click **New**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Type the code">
    Type the account's full code and leave the field. Fail Fast fills in:
    - the account **level**;
    - the **account it adds up into**, with its code and name;
    - the **account class**;
    - a suggested **nature** (debit or credit) based on the class, which you can change.

    If the code already exists, the form opens that account for you to edit. To create a new account, close the form and use a different code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name it">
    Write a name anyone in your company understands, such as **Caja General** or **Clientes Nacionales**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose ledger or auxiliary">
    Choose ledger if you are going to create accounts below it. Choose auxiliary if this is the account you will post to. A ledger account requires nothing on postings, so the section covering what the account requires on each movement is hidden.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set what the account requires on each movement">
    Auxiliary accounts only. Mark what every posting to this account must carry:

    | Field | Use it when |
    |---|---|
    | **Require tax** and **Tax concept** | The account records a tax, such as deductible VAT or a withholding. When you set it to **Yes**, pick the concept. |
    | **Require stakeholder** | You need to know which customer, supplier, or employee each amount belongs to — receivables and payables, for example. |
    | **Require cost center** | You want to see expenses or income by area or project. Setting it to **Yes** also sets branch office to **Yes**, because every movement takes its branch from its cost center. |
    | **Require branch office** | You need the amount by branch. It is only requested together with the cost center. |
    | **Require fclass** | The account has to separate amounts by financial class. |
    | **Require business unit**, **Require principal** and **Restrict linked** | These appear only if your company uses them. |
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the statement item">
    Set which line of the financial statements the account is presented under, such as **Cash and cash equivalents**. Financial indicators and reports use it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    On save, Fail Fast confirms the code is free and the account is correctly placed in the chart. If something is missing, the message tells you what to fix.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Messages you may see when creating an account

| Message (it starts like this) | What to do |
|---|---|
| "The code … already belongs to account …" | The form opens that account for editing. If you wanted a new one, close it and use another code. |
| "The account level must be greater than …, the last level of the global chart" | That level belongs to the general chart. Add digits to the code to create your account below it. |
| "Account … is auxiliary (it receives postings) and cannot have…" | An auxiliary account cannot have accounts below it. Pick a code under a ledger account. |
| "No account in the chart starts the code …" | Create the account it should add up into first, then this one. |
| "Levels are missing between account … and its parent …" | Use the level the message suggests, or create the intermediate account first. |
| "Parent account … is inactive" | Activate the account it adds up into first. |
| "An account requires a branch office only when it requires a cost center…" | Set **Require cost center** to **Yes**, or leave **Require branch office** at **No**. |

## Edit, deactivate, or delete an account

- **Edit.** Open the account from the list and change what you need. Code and level cannot be changed.
- **Switch from ledger to auxiliary.** Only possible if no account adds up into it. An account a book owns cannot become a ledger account; see [Book-owned accounts](/finance/book-own-accounts).
- **Deactivate.** An account cannot be deactivated while it has active accounts below it, or while an active [mapping by book](/finance/account-book-mapping) uses it as the target account. It also cannot be activated if the account it adds up into is inactive.
- **Delete.** Only an account that was never used can be deleted. If it has movements, accounts below it, or a book uses it, the system will not delete it: deactivate it instead.

## Load the chart of accounts from Excel

To load many accounts at once, use the data migration assistant and pick the chart of accounts template. See [Import and export data](/data/import-export).

The load applies the same rules as the form: what the screen will not let you save, the file does not accept either.

- Each row is one account, with its code, name, level, nature, and what it requires on each movement. The template describes the rule behind each column and ships an example that passes every validation.
- **You can put a ledger account and its children in the same file.** Accounts are created level by level, so an account finds the account it adds up into even when that one comes in the same file.
- The account class and the account it adds up into come from the code, exactly as on the form.
- If the account requires tax, write the tax concept code in the **tax_concept_code** column, for example `IVA`.
- An account that already exists is updated, and keeps whether it is a ledger or an auxiliary account.
- A ledger account is stored requiring nothing on movements, even if the file says otherwise.

### What the load rejects

| Reason | What to do |
|---|---|
| The code has characters other than letters and digits | Fix the code |
| The code already belongs to the general chart | Add digits to create your account below it |
| The level is at or below the last level of the general chart | Move the account one level further down the tree |
| The same code appears more than once in the file | Leave one row per account |
| It requires a cost center without a branch office, or the reverse | They go together: set both to **Yes** or both to **No** |
| The account it adds up into failed in the same file | Fix that row first; the child row tells you which one it is |

Errors come back row by row, with the row number from your sheet and the reason written in your language. A badly written yes/no or level does not bring down the whole file: it is reported on its row and the rest carry on.

## Multi-book

If your workspace keeps more than one accounting book, your chart of accounts can read differently in each one. [Multi-book accounting](/finance/multi-book) explains how, and which screens configure it.

## Period control

- **Blocked periods** — a blocked period rejects any new posting into that month, protecting figures you have already reported.
- **Month close** — you close accounting per year and month. Closing is the formal end of the period.
- **One close per book** — if you keep several books, each one closes its months separately; see [Multi-book accounting](/finance/multi-book#period-control).
- **Correcting a closed period** — postings into a blocked or closed period are rejected. Reopen or unblock the period before correcting.

<Warning>
Closing the accounting month blocks further postings in that period. Make sure all operational modules have finished recording the month — invoices, payments, depreciation, inventory costing — before you close.
</Warning>

## Reports

- **Ledger** — balances by account for a period.
- **Auxiliary** — the detailed movement of a specific account (account detail).
- **Movement detail** — entry-level detail across accounts.
- **Withholding certificates** (certificados de retención) — the certificates your company issues to third parties for taxes withheld.

## What posts here

Operational modules generate their accounting automatically: sales, purchases, point of sale, treasury, inventory costing, fixed assets, and transport all post entries to the ledger. Manual journal entries cover everything that does not originate in an operational module.

## Common tasks

<Steps>
  <Step title="Record a manual journal entry">
    Open the journal entries page in your Programs menu and create a new entry through its form. Add the detail lines — account, amounts, and tax details where they apply — and save. The entry posts to the selected book and period.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the accounting month">
    Confirm that every operational module has finished posting for the month, then run the month close for the year and month. Once closed, the period no longer accepts postings.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate a withholding certificate">
    Open the withholding certificates report, select the third party and the period, and generate the certificate (certificado de retención) to deliver to them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How accounting connects to other modules

- **Treasury** — payments, income, transfers, and notes post their entries here; see [Treasury](/finance/treasury).
- **Fixed assets** — every depreciation run posts its entries; see [Fixed assets](/finance/fixed-assets).
- **Operations** — sales, purchasing, POS, and inventory costing feed the ledger continuously.
- **Reporting** — ledger figures feed the dashboards and reports you build in [Dashboards and reports](/reporting/dashboards-and-reports).
