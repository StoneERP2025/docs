Not every company configures the same. The system uses defaults, the RUT, and its own calculations so that a small company fills in almost nothing, and leaves full control to the one that needs it.

The general rule: **fill in only what your company's rules read.** The **Tax profile** tells you when something is missing, with **Missing, required for suppliers**.

| Company | What characterizes it | What it fills in |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro** | Natural person or Simple regime, sells to consumers | Almost nothing |
| **Small** | A VAT-responsible company that does not withhold | Its profile, its CIIU, and its product types |
| **Medium** | Income and ICA withholding agent | What the small one does, plus its suppliers' profile and withholding accounts |
| **Large** | Large taxpayer or self-withholder, with several cities | Everything: profile, activity per municipality, account map, exceptions |

## Micro: the bakery

A bakery sells to people with an ID card and buys from small suppliers. It does not need to fill in a tax profile stakeholder by stakeholder.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Your company">
    Load your RUT or answer your responsibilities in the **Tax profile** of your own company: for example, whether you are in the Simple regime. Register your CIIU and mark it as principal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your product types">
    Choose the tax category and the withholding category of each type. A bread taxed at the general rate and an excluded good are different categories.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your account map">
    The VAT accounts and, if you buy with withholding, the withholding ones. See [Tax account map](/finance/tax-account-map).
  </Step>
</Steps>

**What you do not fill in:**

- The Tax profile of your customers who are persons: without a RUT, the system uses the defaults for persons, and the invoice comes out with VAT without you answering anything. A customer who is a person is not a withholding agent, so there are no withholdings.
- The one of your small suppliers, except the rows the table marks as **Missing, required for suppliers**.
- Exceptions, ICA activities, and periodicity.

With that, a sale to a person with an ID card or to an end consumer settles VAT and nothing else.

<Tip>
If the bakery is in the Simple regime, the special income self-withholding does not arise.
</Tip>

## Small

A company that is VAT responsible and is not a withholding agent.

| Fills in | Where |
|---|---|
| Its responsibilities: VAT responsible, income tax filer | Its company **Tax profile**, or its RUT |
| Its CIIU, with the principal marked | Company record |
| The tax category and withholding category of its product types | Product types |
| The sales CIIU of each type, if it has several activities | Product types |
| The VAT accounts, the withholding accounts that are applied to it, and the self-withholding account if it applies | Tax account map |

Its large customers withhold from it: for that, the customer is the one who needs to be a withholding agent in their own profile. The small company only needs the account map with the **Withholding they make on me** rows so that the withholding is posted.

A small company that files income tax, with a principal CIIU in a self-withholding group, causes the special self-withholding: it needs the map row with role **Generated**, counter account, and return account.

## Medium

A company that withholds income tax and ICA.

Everything the small one does, and also:

| Fills in | Where |
|---|---|
| That it is an income withholding agent and, if it applies, VAT | Its company **Tax profile** |
| That it is an ICA withholding agent in each municipality where it is | **Tax profile**, one row per municipality with zone |
| The **Withheld** accounts of each tax | Tax account map |
| The data of each supplier that its rules read: files income tax, is a self-withholder, simple regime, ICA activity | The supplier's **Tax profile**, or the RUT |
| The ICA activity and periodicity of suppliers in the municipalities where it withholds | The supplier's **Tax profile** |

When a supplier changes activity or municipality, update their Tax profile.

## Large

A large taxpayer or self-withholding company, operating in several cities.

Everything the medium one does, and also:

| Fills in | Where |
|---|---|
| That it is a large taxpayer or an income self-withholder | Its company **Tax profile** |
| Its ICA activity and periodicity in each municipality where it sells | **Tax profile**, one row per municipality |
| An ICA withholding agent row for each municipality that named it an agent | **Tax profile**, with zone |
| An account map with rows per municipality where it needs different accounts | Tax account map, with zone |
| Tax rule override per customer or supplier with agreed rates | Tax rule override |
| The sales CIIU and the purchase ICA activity of each product type | Product types |

A large company reviews the **Missing** rows of the Tax profiles before each close.

## How to know which one you are

There is no sales threshold. Look at whether your company **withholds**: if it does not, you are micro or small. If it withholds income tax and ICA, you are medium. If you are also a large taxpayer, a self-withholder, or sell in several cities, you are large. Moving from one case to the next only adds data; what you already filled in stays valid.
